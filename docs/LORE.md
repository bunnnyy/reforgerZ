# Setting: The Zone

The story and the people of ReforgerZ. How the systems work is in [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md); what each
faction gives and takes is in [REPUTATION.md](REPUTATION.md); how their lines are written (the tone,
the voices, the rules every line follows) is in [WRITING.md](WRITING.md).

## The premise

Eleven days ago something got out. Within forty-eight hours the government
declared the region a **Quarantine Zone** and sealed it off: roadblocks
on every road out, drones over the line, patrol boats on the water, snipers
nobody ever sees. No one gets in. **No one gets out.**

The official broadcast still loops on every frequency:

> *"This area is under federal quarantine. Remain indoors. Do not approach the
> perimeter. Aid is on its way."*

Aid is not on its way. Nothing comes in, and nothing ever will. You're a civilian who was
here when the gates closed. Everyone in the Zone is stuck in it together: the soldiers who
were left inside, the ordinary people who happened to be here, the people who saw their chance
in it, and the scientists who might be the only way it ends.

**The only way the quarantine ends is a cure.**

## Every system has a reason

| System | In the Zone |
|---|---|
| Map edge | **The Perimeter.** An unseen cordon: drones, loudspeakers and snipers you never see. Get close and you're warned. Keep going and you're shot. |
| Task Force Bastion | Soldiers who were inside when the order came. Command left them here to "hold the line". By Day 5 they knew nobody was coming, and they closed ranks: they're all they have. |
| The Haven | Ordinary people stuck in the Zone who decided to help each other, because nobody outside will. |
| The Red Hand | People who saw the broken world as their chance. Every man for himself, and everyone knows anyone would sell them. |
| Halcyon | A research team that was studying the outbreak site before the lockdown. Only they can prove the Zone is safe, so the world outside will lift the quarantine. |
| Outbreak stages | How long the Zone has been sealed. |
| Last Helicopter | Bastion's one working helicopter, shuttling between its own bases *inside* the Zone. Nobody outside resupplies anyone, Bastion included. |
| Loot | Nothing comes in from outside. Everything you have, you scavenged, earned or took. |
| Supply trucks | Everything that moves inside the Zone moves by road. A lost truck means empty shelves. |
| The cure | Not just a cure: it's **proof**. Deliver it and the quarantine is lifted. |

## The factions, as people

### The Haven

The Haven are the people who happened to be here: a bus driver whose route ended at a
roadblock, a coach of tourists, a teacher with the eleven kids from her class trip, a
night-shift nurse, farmers, retirees. Nobody planned a faction.

It started with strangers. On Day 2 someone forced the doors of a school gym and let
people sleep there. On Day 4 a priest opened his church. Word went round by radio and
by notes on doors, and more places opened. The Haven is **a network, not an army**: a
handful of safehouses that talk to each other. The rule for getting in is simple: *you get
in if you're willing to help.*

**How a safehouse runs.** A council runs each one: whoever stepped up, usually the person
who opened the door. Every house keeps the same rules:

- You can carry and draw your weapon. **You don't fire it** near the house.
- **Everyone works**: watch, water, cooking, burying.
- **Declare your bite.** Say it out loud, and the house will help if it can.

**The Haven remembers its own.** When someone who stood up for the Haven dies, every house
lights a candle in the window and the council reads the name out at supper. Whoever turns up
later with the same look about them is fed, and seen by the medic, for nothing. Someone the
Haven has come to trust is given an amber ribbon, to wear or not as they like
([REPUTATION.md](REPUTATION.md), "Reputation clues").

**The bear.** On Day 2 the teacher's eleven kids slept in the gym, and somebody stencilled a
teddy bear on its doors so that nobody would shoot through them. Every safehouse with children
in it has the bear on its door now, and the Haven uses it as its mark. It has been touched up so
often that it has a patch on its belly and one eye stitched shut.

**The lodger.** On Day 2 a woman was on the bus Sedlak turned round at the north road. On Day 4 she
was bitten in the church doorway, the morning the priest opened it, and declared it, as every house
asks. The priest sat up with her all night, and in the morning she asked for tea. On Day 6 she was
meant to walk up the hill with the volunteers; her council head kept her back, and the lab's ledger
says *"did not present"*. Then her house crossed her off the List with the pencil it uses for the
dead, so the lab wouldn't come for her and Bastion couldn't count her: the one name on the List
crossed out as a kindness. Since then she's moved from house to house at dusk, a mouth on half
portions in a network whose rule is that everyone works. Her son runs with a Red Hand crew. Nobody
says why she didn't turn. *"They crossed me off to keep me safe. Nobody asked me either."*

**The hard edges.** The Haven is kind, and it is also frightened and hungry. It has a
little dark side that nobody talks about:

| What they don't say out loud | What it means | Where it shows in play |
|---|---|---|
| **The List** | Every house keeps a list of names. Some are quietly crossed out, and nobody says why. | Rumours and notes; The List contract (why one name was crossed out, and who gets told); the questline The List (whose pencil it is) |
| **"Walking someone out"** | What happens to someone who declared a bite. It differs by house: in one they sit with you until the end; in another someone walks you to the edge of town at dusk and comes back alone. | The Walk Him Out contract; the questline The Edge of Town (the walks against the lab) |
| **"Mouths"** | When food runs low, people who can't work become "mouths", and mouths get smaller portions. | Half portions in the news; the Quiet Congregation arc |
| **Exile** | Break the rules badly enough and you're put out at night, without your weapon. | Exile when the Haven hunts you |
| **Quiet arrangements** | Some houses pay the Red Hand to be left alone. Sometimes the price is a stranger: someone the house turned out, and where to find them. | A Quiet Arrangement; a hungry house selling an exile's location; the questline The Price of the Door |
| **"Borrowing"** | Taking from houses that weren't empty. Taking from a better-fed safehouse. Always in quotes. | Haven gossip in the news |

**How they see the others:**

- **Bastion** is useful and frightening. They have medicine and guns, and they have orders.
- **The Red Hand** were ordinary people once. Some of them used to come to the same church.
- **Halcyon** calls the infected "samples". The Haven calls them by name when it can.

**Voice:** warm, worried, practical, sometimes darkly funny. *"Bring water if you can. Bring
yourself either way."*

### Task Force Bastion

Soldiers who were inside the line when the order came: a battalion on exercise in the hills,
the first cordon detail sent in to "contain an incident", reservists, a few men on leave who
reported for duty anyway. Command told them to hold the line and stopped answering. By Day 5
they knew nobody was coming. The people they love are on the other side of the line, and the
people on the roster are all they have. Bastion closed ranks around them, and whatever that
costs, somebody else usually pays.

It started with orders that made sense. On Day 1 the cordon detail put roadblocks round an
"incident" in the hills and was told it was a two-week posting. At six on the morning of Day 2
the order came to walk civilians to the road checkpoints at the edge, to be processed out.
Lieutenant Hana Sedlak took a bus, forty cars and a line of people on foot to the checkpoint
on the north road. At two in the afternoon the barrier was still down. A loudspeaker told them
to turn back. The soldiers behind the concrete wore the same badge she did. When she walked
up to talk to them, a warning shot went through the bus's mirror. She turned the column round.
The bus driver's route ended there, and he went looking for somewhere to put his passengers.

That evening the last clear message came over the net: *"Hold the line. Await relief."*
On Day 3 a longer signal came through the relay, too broken for voice, and a runner set off
with the decoded text. He never arrived. That night the senior officer inside, a major, gave
the units a name on the radio, Task Force Bastion, so that Command would have something to
call them. Command never used it. The same night a pilot flew for the line with the wounded
aboard. The line shot him down short of it. Nobody has flown toward the edge since, and of
the helicopters Bastion came in with, only one still flies.

On Day 4 they were still waiting, and Sedlak put two men with binoculars on the hill over the
north road. At 05:30 on Day 5 Corporal Radek Vrana watched four trucks with his own badge on the
doors come up the far side of the barrier. Fresh men took over the sandbags, the men who had
turned Sedlak's column back climbed aboard, and the trucks drove away: a relief in place, by the
manual, in eleven minutes. Nobody on the far side looked up the hill. Sedlak wrote in the log:
*"05:41. Relief observed, north road barrier. Not for us."* That night the major read it to
Command with the numbers. Signaller Alena Holub waited a long time, then wrote *"No reply"*. At
the shift change on Day 6 the major, who is still Bastion's commanding officer, spoke to every
post, and the operators wrote it down word for word:

> *"All stations, Bastion. Yesterday at 05:41 the north road barrier was relieved in place.
> Nobody came for us. Nobody is coming. From now on we feed our own, we look for our own, and we
> bring our own home, one way or the other. Nobody walks out on the rest. The roster stands. The
> call stands. Bastion out."*

That evening Sedlak, who keeps the roster at the stronghold, wrote onto it the families from her
Day 2 column who had stayed with the soldiers: *"Civilians, attached."* They stand no watch, and
when one of them doesn't come back, the stronghold goes looking, the same as for a soldier.

Sergeant Bohdan Zeman had eleven men at a post past the hills, three days of rations and a truck
that hadn't come. On Day 7 they took the first "contribution" from the village below. A farmer
who wouldn't open his barn was knocked down and didn't get up, and the log says *"Infected,
engaged at the wire."* That night Private Marek Fiala, who had been at the barn, walked out with a
week of what came out of it, and two men from his section went after him. On Day 8 a Red Hand toll
on the hill road left Private Ivo Brabec dead in the ditch. After dark a man from the next crew's
road brought the toll crew's camp to the wire on a cigarette packet, and the post went out without
orders. By morning the lorry was burning and three of its crew were in the stockade.

On Day 9 a squad came from the stronghold to count Zeman's books. Its sergeant read the log,
looked at the thin faces and the new tag on the nail, left half his squad's rations behind and
signed it all correct. That evening Fiala's section came back with the rations and without him,
and the board says *"Private Fiala. KIA Day 7. Body not recovered."* In ten days he was the only
one to walk out. On Day 10 the stronghold took a contribution too, and Zeman was bitten on a
contribution run. There was no diesel to get him to the lab, so his corporal sat with him. Every
post since has run somewhere between Sedlak's roster and Zeman's barn, and its commander's traits
say which way it leans ([REPUTATION.md](REPUTATION.md#traits)).

**Held for collection.** The order on Day 2 said to screen the civilians first, *"with the samples to
go along with them"*. At a tent short of the north road barrier the battalion's medical officer took
a tube from everyone in Sedlak's column, three hundred people or so; Sedlak rolled up her sleeve first
so the column would. Every tube got a number, and every person a slip with the number and their name,
stapled to the bus driver's clipboard for the barrier. The captain's log has two lines that afternoon:
*"13:10. Results to Command by radio: 4 R / {count}. Ack."* and *"14:00. Barrier not lifted. Column
turned back."* Nobody says what anyone outside did with that number in the fifty minutes between. The
samples had nobody left to go with, so he wrote *"Held for collection"* and took them in two chest
freezers to the battalion's cold store in the hills. He's still there. The roster says *"Detached,
stores: 1."* He opens to "collection", only the CO can say it, and a tube goes only with its slip.

**Twice.** Private Marek Fiala was on Sedlak's cordon detail on Day 1, bitten through the sleeve,
and never turned. He gave his tube with the column on Day 2 (R2: one of the four marked R). He's the
man who walked out of Zeman's post on Day 7, and the board still says he was killed in action. He
runs with the Red Hand crew nearest the cold store now, and the crews call him Twice: *"Killed in
action once. Bitten once. Neither took."* He keeps out of Bastion's sight, because he knows someone is
sent to bring a struck-off man home. Bastion's word for a deserter is "struck off"; the book's word
for a cleared line is "paid".

**How a post runs.** A commander runs each post: an officer if there's one left, a sergeant
if not ([Who's in charge](#whos-in-charge)). Every post keeps the same routine:

- **The roster.** Every name, every duty, in grease pencil on a board. The shift changes at 05:30
  and 17:30, and the night watch starts at 20:30. A name on it eats, stands a watch if it can, and
  is looked for if it doesn't come back (Missing Patrol, Rescue the Pilot). Anyone can be signed
  on: a recruit (Training Day), a mechanic, a family. The dead stay on it. The only thing anyone
  rubs out is a KIA, when a man comes back (Struck Off).
- **The roll.** Since Day 6, when the night watch is set, the sergeant reads the whole roster out
  loud. The living answer *"Here."* For each of the dead, the whole post answers together. The
  roll gets longer every week.
- **The nightly call.** Then the operator reads the numbers to Command: effective strength,
  rounds, fuel, civilians sheltered, and last, *"Requesting relief."* It's how the call has always
  ended, and no post wants to be the first to stop. Every post hears the others and knows it isn't
  the last one left, and a post that reads *"rations, one day"* usually has a truck from the
  stronghold by morning. Then the operator writes *"No reply"*, and the sergeant chalks up a day.
- **Inside the wire,** weapons are slung and nobody fires. Outside it, civilians are searched at
  the checkpoints and there's a curfew at dusk.
- **Everything is signed for.** Ammunition, rations, fuel, people.

No post breaks away, even one that has stopped taking the stronghold's orders: on its own is the
one thing Bastion is afraid of. On the secure net, the stronghold answers to "Command" now.
Someone had to.

**Bastion counts its dead.** They're buried outside the post with no salute, because they're
saving ammunition. One tag stays with the body. The other goes on a nail behind the radio, under
the name, and the name gets a "KIA". That night the operator reads the new names to Command.
Promotions come off the dead too: the new corporal's stripes were cut from a dead man's sleeve,
and nobody thinks less of them for it. **Nobody turns alone.** A bitten soldier goes to the lab for
the trial dose with one friend. If it doesn't take, or there's no diesel to get there, that friend
sits with them and is the one who ends it. A civilian who has earned Bastion's trust is waved
through at the checkpoints, and a few are trusted to throw the smoke the guns answer to
([REPUTATION.md](REPUTATION.md), "Task Force Bastion").

**The hard edges.** Bastion looks after its own, and it doesn't ask what that costs anyone else.
Underneath the discipline, it does things nobody puts in the log:

| What they don't say out loud | What it means | Where it shows in play |
|---|---|---|

**How they see the others:**

- **The Haven** are the people Bastion walked to the line and back on Day 2. The Haven takes in
  anyone willing to help; Bastion feeds its own first, and each thinks the other's way gets people
  killed. Every post takes a little from the Haven, and some send a crate back against orders
  (Hearts and Minds).
- **The Red Hand** are looters with a logo, and worse: people who'd sell the one next to them. A
  crew that kills a soldier is sold to Bastion by the crew next door, and Bastion takes the map
  and despises whoever brought it. They laugh at Bastion on the open channel; Bastion keeps a list.
- **Halcyon** has the lab Command wanted secured, and Bastion thinks it knew what was coming
  before anyone. The cure is the one thing that opens the gates, and everyone Bastion loves is on
  the other side of them. The major wants it in a locked crate, so that Bastion's own are dosed
  first ([Authors](QUESTLINES.md#hc3-authors)). Then the roster will have to say who they are: it
  carries civilians, attached, and the two men who stayed at the lab on Day 5, detached. Nobody
  says what happens to the roster when the gates open.

**Voice:** clipped, procedural, tired, and close with its own. *"Command, Bastion. Effective
strength thirty-eight. Civilians sheltered, two hundred and eleven. Requesting relief. Over."* A
long hiss. *"Bastion out."* At a checkpoint: *"Hands where I can see them. I don't know you, so
you get searched."*

### The Red Hand

People who looked at the broken world and saw their chance. Some were criminals before; most were
mechanics, drivers, a used-car salesman, a bailiff, whoever ran the card games behind the bowling
alley, and bored young men who worked out first that nobody was coming and nothing taken would ever
have to be given back. They take tolls on the roads, run a black market and sell protection, mostly
from themselves. It's every man for himself, and they're the ones who say so.

It started with a lie. On Day 2 the cars that had been turned back from the line queued at every
petrol station, and the pumps ran dry. Rudi Kasper owned a garage at a crossroads, and the key to
the tank under its forecourt. He told the queue it was empty, and that night sold it round the back
to the same people, by the can: for money, then tins, then wedding rings. One of them had been at
the front of that queue all day, and had nothing left to pay with. Rudi let them have ten litres on
tick and wrote it in the back of his order book. That was the boss, and it's the first line in the
book: *"Day 2. Ten litres, on tick."*

From Day 3 the boss kept Rudi's tally, and wrote down more than diesel: who came round the back,
what they paid with, and whose it had been. Rudi gave his new partner a bed in the office. On Day 4
the Janda brothers, his mechanics, parked an articulated lorry across the crossroads with two
shotguns on top, and the first toll was one tin a car. The four of them shook on it: Rudi's key,
the brothers' lorry, the boss's book.

The convicts, out since the guards went home on Day 1, drifted in that week, and then anyone who'd
noticed that taking was quicker than queueing. Rudi's share was the biggest, because the diesel was
his, and he still let people he knew pay later. On Day 7 the boss told the brothers that Rudi had
been bitten and was hiding it. He hadn't been. He rolled up his sleeves and asked them to look, and
nobody looked: they'd been given a reason they could live with, and a bigger share. They took the
tank key and his boots, put him out of his own garage at dusk and held the shutter down. He
hammered on it for a long time. By morning it was true.

The boss painted one red hand over the marks Rudi's hands had left on the shutter, said it meant
stop, and wrote *"Paid"* against the first line in the book. Under Rudi's last sum, in the same hand:
*"Day 7. R. Kasper. Bitten."* The shutter is still at the crossroads, and the crews call it Rudi's. The tank key went on a new belt, and
from then on diesel went only to lorries with a red hand on them, for a cut of the toll. The lorry
on the hill road had its hand by noon. By Day 10 every crossroads that wanted diesel had one.

The brothers had held the shutter down together. On Day 9 the boss told each of them, alone, that
the other had offered to sell him. The younger believed it first and said his brother had been
skimming the toll, and on Day 10 the older Janda went into the first pit for what the book said he
owed. Nobody checked the book. By then half a dozen crew bosses had argued for two days about who
was in charge. The boss read each of them their own line, quietly: what they'd paid with, and whose
it had been. The arguing stopped. The younger Janda runs the pit now, and doesn't drink with
anyone.

**The cook.** She cooks the dusk meal at the stronghold, and ran the school kitchen in town until
Day 2; the crews call her Doc because she isn't one. On Day 2 she queued at Rudi's forecourt all day,
and that night he sold her forty litres round the back and let her pay later, because he knew her.
Her line is on Rudi's first page, under the boss's ten litres. On Day 7 she took Rudi his supper the
evening the boss told the brothers he'd been bitten: *"He let people pay later. That's what he died
of."* Rudi's tally became the boss's book, every "pay later" in it became owed to the boss, and she's
paid hers off in the kitchen ever since. After supper she cooks the other thing, on an old farm still
in a barn nobody lives near: the cut dose. Beside the book she keeps her own pages, which pan each
line's dose came from. The Red Hand ran the biggest trial in the Zone and wrote the misses up as
debts. The crews take their own cut only when a man's worth less than a real dose, and every man
knows it.

**How it runs.** A crew holds a camp and a stretch of road, and a crew boss runs it. Every crew
pays **the boss** a cut. The boss hardly needs to give orders, because the boss holds **the book**:
everything owed, who owes it and what for, and under some debts what somebody did and who saw. The
boss also runs **the market** at the stronghold (stolen kit, the cut dose, a fence who buys watches
and rings by the handful). On the roads there's **the toll**: tins, fuel or something worth
knowing, mostly tins. The book has to balance, which isn't the same as anyone being paid up.

**What holds it together** is that anyone in the Red Hand would sell anyone, and everyone knows it,
so everyone keeps something on everyone else. A crew boss with a page in the book holds the boss's
line; one who thinks there's no page on them runs the road their own way; and there's always one
selling the boss to somebody ([Who's in charge](#whos-in-charge)).

**The code** has five rules, and nobody thinks they're about honour:

- **Pay your cut.** Crews that skim get found out, usually because another crew sold them.
- **A debt is a debt.** It stays in the book until it's paid, by someone. Dying doesn't pay it.
- **Guns stay quiet in camp.** Shots bring the infected, and witnesses. Do it outside.
- **Nobody rats.** Everybody does. The rule is for whoever gets caught.
- **Who you were doesn't matter.** Who you've crossed does, and the book remembers.

**The Red Hand sends its own off loud:** horns, music, shots in the air, whatever's left in the
bottle, and more than once a horde has come to see what the fuss was. The louder the send-off, the
fewer people ask how he died. His kit is on the market before the horns stop, and what he owed goes
in the book against whoever he left: his crew, his brother, a woman in a safehouse. Rudi was never
sent off at all.

Status is a **name**, and the crew picks it, usually after something you'd rather they forgot. New
faces paint a red hand on, and look pleased about it. Once the book has something on them, somebody
tells them what's under the first one. It isn't a secret: it's a warning, and nobody can sell the
boss what everybody knows. The red armband goes to the ones the crews trust, which means the crew
has something on you ([REPUTATION.md](REPUTATION.md), "Reputation clues").

**The hard edges.** The Red Hand jokes about everything and says the worst of it out loud. What it
never says is who paid:

| What they don't say out loud | What it means | Where it shows in play |

| **"He was bitten"** | What the Red Hand says about Rudi, and about anyone else it needed gone. Everyone inside knows Rudi wasn't; outside, it's only a rumour. | **Owing** | Nobody is ever out of the book, and that's on purpose: a man who owes nothing can walk away. **How they see the others:**

- **The Haven:** lovely people, lots of tins, terrible locks. They share and go hungry. Some houses
  pay to be left alone, and a house that pays is in the book.
- **Bastion** is the one crew in the Zone nobody can buy a name from: its posts sell rifles off the
  inventory, never a soldier. Kill one of theirs and all of Bastion is at your door (the hill road
  found out on Day 8), so the crews hand Bastion the man who did it before it comes. They break out
  of its stockade only the ones who owe them, and can't work out why soldiers hardly ever sell each
  other.
- **Halcyon** pays in hope; the Red Hand pays in cash, and the lab's doses sell better with its
  stickers on. The cure is worth more than every tin in the Zone, and the boss means to buy the
  batch, or steal it if the lab won't sell ([Authors](QUESTLINES.md#hc3-authors)).

**Voice:** mocking, casual, always counting. They laugh at Bastion on the open channel. *"Red hand
means stop. Stop means pay. Pay means we're friends. See? Easy."* The boss, asked about Rudi: *"He
let people pay later. You can't run a road like that."*

### Halcyon

A research team that was at the outbreak site before the lockdown. They run the only lab.
They pay for samples, they need guards, and they know more about how this started than they
say. A cure is the only thing that opens the gates, for them and for everyone.

It started before Day 1, and that's the problem. Halcyon is the field team of a research
institute, and it had been working for months at a closed site in the hills. What the site
was for is in their reports. Nobody else has read the reports. Three weeks before the gates
closed, a man from the site was brought into a clinic with a fever nobody could name. Someone
wrote *"notify the Institute"* in the margin and underlined it twice.

On Day 1, whatever got out, got out. The team loaded two vans with the freezers, the
centrifuge and every notebook they could find, and drove down into town. On Day 2 the
Institute's last call reached them: nobody leaves until the Zone is proven clean, and proof
opens the gates. Then the line went dead. On Day 3 the team lead, Dr Greta Wolff, went back up
to the site for the rest of the samples. Her driver came back alone and wouldn't say what he'd
seen. On Day 4 a Bastion platoon arrived with Command's order, two days old, to secure the
lab. The researchers wouldn't open the doors, and the soldiers wouldn't leave, and they stood
there all night. In the morning
the platoon went home, except for two men who stayed and became Halcyon's first guards. On Day 6
ten bitten people from the Haven volunteered for the first trial dose. Eight walked out. The
number has been eight in ten ever since, which the lab says is a coincidence.

**The lower store.** At 04:10 on Day 1 the cordon detail chained the closed site's lower store and
pasted an order on it: *"By order: lower store to be secured and not opened. Day 1, 04:10. Lt H.
Sedlak."* Two keys were cut, and both were signed for. On Day 2 the detail went down to walk the
column and never came back up. On Day 3 Dr Wolff went up for the rest of the samples, found the chain,
sent her driver down and stayed with three of her team. Her clipboard still hangs by the lower
store's door: *"Day 3. Chained. Soldiers gone. Four of us staying."* Behind the chain is a dewar with
the first isolate in it, labelled with the clinic patient's surname and the date the Institute was
told. The director has known where it is since Day 3, and has never asked Bastion for the key.

**The eight and the ham.** Whatever the trial dose taught the blood of the eight who walked out on
Day 6, they've carried it longest; most went back to the Haven's houses, and nobody owns them. The
lab has read its numbers on the open channel at every change of shift since, *"in case somebody
outside is writing them down"*. Nobody outside is. A retired schoolteacher with a long-wire aerial
between two trees, in a village nobody else lives in, has copied every one into school exercise
books: *"In case. Somebody should."*

**How it runs.** The research director runs the lab, and the lab never stops:

- **The lab** works in shifts, because somebody always has to watch the incubators. At night
  the generator goes to the freezers and the lights go dim. The overnight numbers are read out
  at the change of shift. Nobody comments on them.
- **The field stations.** A house in a quiet village with sensors, a cold box and a radio, run
  by a lead researcher who is often younger than their job. Couriers walk samples between them
  and the lab in steel cases.
- **The trial dose.** Anyone bitten may volunteer, once, for nothing. Eight in ten walk out,
  and Halcyon writes down what happens to the other two
  ([DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md#treatments)).
- **The notebooks.** Everyone keeps one, numbered. Every reading, every sample, every
  volunteer goes in it. When a researcher dies, the notebook belongs to the lab.
- **The numbers go out on the open channel,** with the samples wanted, in case somebody
  outside is writing them down.

**Halcyon keeps its dead in the record.** When someone dies, the director speaks for two
minutes and goes back to work. Their notebook is read into the record, page by page, and their
name goes on the list of authors for the paper that will open the gates: researchers, couriers,
guards. Volunteers who didn't walk out are on it too, by number. The Haven wants them named, and now and
then somebody carries a name back up the hill.
Status is whose initials go on a result, and who is let into the clean room. An outsider the lab
trusts gets antidotes, a medic, and in the end a pass to the lab itself
([REPUTATION.md](REPUTATION.md), "Halcyon").

**The beaks.** Outside the clean room Halcyon's people wear hoods and respirators with long
filters, and the Zone calls them the beaks. Somebody at the lab stencilled a plague doctor on the
sample cases as a joke. By the time anyone senior noticed, it was on every case, door and van,
and nobody could think of a reason to scrub it off.

**The hard edges.** Halcyon is careful, polite and very tired. It has decided some things
matter more than people, and it apologises for that, quietly:

| What they don't say out loud | What it means | Where it shows in play |
|---|---|---|
| **The three weeks** | The first case was three weeks before the gates closed, and the Institute was told. Nobody at the lab says what anyone did with those three weeks. | Patient Zero (the clinic records); the questline The Three Weeks (the driver who came back alone, the closed site, Dr Wolff's notebook and the Institute's one-word reply, "Continue."); the rumour that Halcyon made this |
| **"The other two"** | The two in ten who don't walk out of the trial dose leave with half the time they came in with, and a page in someone's notebook. Someone sits with them with a clipboard instead of a hand. | The trial dose; Serum Trial; Unsanctioned Trial; the questline The Other Two (a back room at a field station, a Haven woman's brother, and names for the numbers) |
| **"Samples first, people second"** | When a van can't take both, the cold boxes go. It's an order, and they say sorry for it on the secure channel. | Secure channels |
| **"At the moment of turning"** | The protocol wants tissue taken as a person turns, so someone has to be there, waiting, with the kit. | Patients Not Samples; The Other Two (the sample kit by the bed) |
| **Live ones** | Specials caught alive, for the lab, and sometimes for a field lead's own notebook. People say Halcyon pays the Red Hand for live ones. | Live Capture; Private Collection; The Three Weeks (Dr Wolff, brought down alive); rumours |
| **Pages in the stove** | A dead researcher's notebook belongs to the lab. The pages about the director don't always get there. | The Notebook |
| **Lazarus** | A locked terminal from a team that went into the field and didn't come back. Nobody at the lab will say what the project was. | The Last Experiment |

**How they see the others:**

- **The Haven** keeps people alive long enough to matter. Its medics have seen more bites than
  anyone at the lab, and the lab sometimes asks them what it's getting wrong.
- **Bastion** would like the cure in a locked crate. The director would trade what the lab
  knows for Bastion's protection, and some field leads would rather give it away.
- **The Red Hand** buys samples, and every one it buys is research the lab loses. It also sells
  the lab the things the director won't ask for.

**Voice:** precise, a bit cold, sometimes guilty. *"Eight in ten. We write down the other two.
Somebody has to, and they should be counted."*

## The Long Table

How the house works in the world is in [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md#the-long-table); how players deal
with it is in [REPUTATION.md](REPUTATION.md#the-long-table); its two questlines are in
[QUESTLINES.md](QUESTLINES.md#the-long-table).

Not a faction. A family of four to seven people in one farmhouse, in one of the empty places
nobody goes. They're kind to strangers. There's always an extra place set at supper. And they
eat people.

It started with the Haven's mouths. On Day 3 Ada Moravec opened her farmhouse past the last
village to anyone the other houses couldn't feed. She had two grown sons, a daughter-in-law and a
kitchen table long enough for twenty. The council sent her its mouths: the old, the sick, a man in
a wheelchair, two men who couldn't stop coughing, and a boy of ten whose mother never made it to
the gym. On Day 7 the Haven truck to the farm didn't come. On Day 8 the council's radio said there
would be nothing for the farm that week: houses with workers came first. On Day 9 old Mr Halas
died in the night. In the morning there was soup. Ada said grace and read his name out, the way
every Haven house reads its dead at supper. Nobody asked. The ones who would have asked weren't
strong enough to.

On Day 10 one of the coughing men turned out to have a bite he hadn't declared, and by the next
night Ada's daughter-in-law was sick. Her husband walked her out at dusk and came back alone. The
family has asked every guest about bites ever since. By Day 12 the farm had stopped answering the
radio. The council crossed the whole house off the List and said a prayer for it, and everyone
agreed the infected must have got there first.

The mouths are gone now. The family is still there, and the boy.

**How the house runs.** Ada runs the kitchen, and the kitchen is the house:

- **There's always a place set.** An extra bowl at the long table, for whoever comes.
- **Everyone is welcome.** Her elder son, the host, meets travellers on the road with a flask of
  something hot. He knows the roads, the trucks and who's short, and everything he says is true.
- **Declare your bite.** They ask every guest, kindly, the way a Haven house does. A bitten guest
  is fed, blessed and sent on their way.
- **Grace.** Before supper Ada reads a name.
- **Nothing is wasted.** That's the whole of the reason, and they say it often.

They tell themselves they only took the ones who were going anyway. That was true for a week.

**The hard edges.** The house is homely, and that's the horror of it:

| What they don't say out loud | What it means | Where it shows in play |
|---|---|---|
| **Grace** | At a Haven house, the name read at supper is the dead. At Ada's, it's the meal | Every meal at the long table (the name is the last one who never arrived); A Place at the Table |
| **"Nothing is wasted"** | It started with the dead. Then the dying. Then the ones nobody would come looking for | Wanderers who never arrive; rumours |
| **"Declare your bite"** | Asked kindly, like a Haven house. A bitten guest is safe: they learned on Day 10 what the bitten do to a family | A bitten player is never set on; Halcyon's interest |
| **The guestbook** | Every guest signs. Some names have a date and a small cross beside them, the way the List does | Proof in The House That Went Quiet |
| **The boots** | A row of boots by the door, in too many sizes | A tell at the house |
| **"Pork"** | What the jars are, when they're sold. The one Red Hand crew that buys them doesn't ask | Trade; Cargo |
| **Strays** | Some of the people the Haven turned out, that the Red Hand kept track of, were sold on here | Cargo; The Price of the Door |
| **The boy** | He isn't theirs. He came with the mouths. He eats what he's given and doesn't talk about it | The end of the house; the Kid wanderer |

**How the others see them** (once they know):

- **The Haven:** horror, and shame. It was one of their houses, and they sent it their mouths and
  then sent it nothing. The Haven calls it "the house that went quiet" and never says more. Its
  instinct is to bring them home, and walk them out.
- **Bastion:** a line in a report: an infected cluster past the last village, cleared. It doesn't
  fit anywhere on the board, so the commanding officer would rather burn it than count it. A
  post or two has bought venison from a man with a flask.
- **The Red Hand:** the code has five rules and none of them is about this. The boss says it
  doesn't need one. One crew buys the jars, calls it pork and sells the farm strays; most crews
  would hang the family off the lorry if they knew.
- **Halcyon:** interested, and ashamed of being interested. The family never eats a bitten guest,
  because of Day 10. The lab would like to know if they're right: whether eating the infected
  spreads it. It would like one of them alive to ask, and a sample to be sure.

**Voice:** homely, attentive, a little too interested in you. *"Sit, sit. You're thin. Where've you
come from, and does anyone know you're out this way?"* Then grace: *"For what we are about to
receive. For Tomas, who came from Levie."*

**Writing them.** Never describe it. No kitchen work, no cellar contents, no word for what they
are, from them or from the narrator. The horror is in ordinary things: the extra bowl, the boots,
the names, the jars labelled by the day and not by what's in them. Other people may joke (the Red
Hand says "pork"); the writing never does.

## What the infected do

The setting behind the infected. How they work in the game is in [INFECTED.md](INFECTED.md); how
they're written is in [WRITING.md](WRITING.md#writing-the-infected). The docs and the code call the slow ones
crawlers, the packs tribes and their leaders alphas; the game never does.

### What is true

This is for the author. The game never says all of it.

It spreads by bite, and the bitten turn. How someone turns decides what they become:

| How they turned | What they become | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Lying still: in bed with the fever, in a cellar, in a locked bathroom, in a car with the doors shut | **One of the slow ones.** Swollen, in the town where it turned, in the house or the street next to it | The infection builds whatever the body was doing when it took. Lying still, it builds weight. They drift to noise because noise was always people |
| Running: bitten on a road, caught in a field, walked out to the edge of town at dusk, or got up and ran when the fever broke | **One of a pack.** Fast and lean, and never far from others | Running, it builds legs and lungs, and something in them wants a lead to follow |
| Running, early, and kept feeding | **A leader.** Big and heavy, slower to tire than to anger | It gets bigger every week it leads and feeds. It calls at dusk, and the ones in earshot come to it |

That one fact explains the rest:

| What players see | Why |
|---|---|
| Towns full of slow ones, the wild full of fast ones | The same sickness, taking two kinds of people |
| A pack gets bigger next to a town that's going quiet | Not everyone who turned in a town turned lying down. The ones who ran went where the calling was |
| A few of them out by day, in twos and threes | The young and the hungry can't lie still all day. They walk the paths the pack walks at night, and come back before the call |
| The dusk call | The leader calls its people in before the hunt. A pack in earshot answers from its own ridge |
| The call changes | Hungry ones call long. Fed ones sleep. An old one's voice goes ragged. After a death there are many voices and no lead |
| Borders | Two calls that answer each other mark where one pack's land ends. Sound carries badly over water and down a valley, so rivers and valley floors are borders, and bridges are fought over |
| The same trails every night | They go where they found people before |
| The dawn walk home | Home is wherever the leader lies up |
| The young are erratic | They're new. Some still go toward the houses they lived in |
| The vigil | When the call stops, they wait by it |
| Succession | The next strongest starts calling. The rest decide whether to follow |
| Splits | Twenty-five can't all hear one voice, so somebody at the far end starts calling |
| Fear of a place | They don't go back where a lot of them died, for a while |
| One long call in the night | The leader calling them off |
| The slow ones come out after a hunt | There was a lot of noise first, and something is left |
| Nothing near the line | The snipers shoot whatever walks into it. *"Even they don't go near the line."* |

### Nothing is born

No breeding, and nothing from outside. Every infected was somebody inside the Zone: a resident, a
soldier, a driver, a volunteer, a Haven runner, a player's character.
- A pack grows only from people: those it takes, and those who ran when their town went quiet. So a
  pack is a map of where people went.
- A nest of the slow ones (Burn the Nest, Specimen from the Nest) is where a town's slow ones lie up
  thickest, breathing in time. The Silent Village's *"It hums. You can hear it in your teeth."* is a
  nest.

### Echoes

A pack keeps a little of whoever leads it, and nobody explains it.
- A pack led by a soldier sets out on the 17:30 shift change. At 20:30 one of its guard stands at the
  lair's edge like a night watch.
- When a farmer takes over, the pack starts coming home at first light the long way round, by the
  fields.
- The pack of the Day 2 column still walks toward the line at dawn, and stops short of it.

### The site's own

The oldest pack in the Zone ranges round the closed site in the hills. Who its leader was is a canon
key the lab never says (`tribe.site.who`). Dr Wolff stays exactly as The Three Weeks has her: one
turned researcher, alone in the store. The driver's *"she stayed because she wanted to"* still reads
either way.

### Ordinary people, small stories

- **The column.** Not everyone Sedlak turned round on Day 2 made it back, and the first pack was made
  on that road. On an island with no road out, the north road ends at the water: the column waited on
  the slipway, and the loudspeaker was on a boat.
- **Bastion's lost posts** don't empty. Their men are still on the roster, the post still answers for
  them at the roll, and they still keep the shift change.
- **A runner's bicycle** lies in the ditch on the Levie road, its back wheel still turning.
- **The young come home.** Sometimes they stand at the edge of town at dusk and look at the door with
  the bear on it.
- **A town going quiet.** *"Half of Levie went quiet this week. Not all of them stayed."*

### What people say

Players only ever get guesses, and nobody is marked right. Every line names the place, so players can
tell four names mean one pack. The words each faction uses in the game, and its marks, are in
[INFECTED.md](INFECTED.md#the-words); as people:

| Who | Their words | How they see them |
|---|---|---|
| Halcyon | Pack P-02, the index, recent conversions, sessile cases, an isolate. The Zone calls Halcyon's people the beaks, after the plague doctor on their plates | Numbered packs and an index it wants alive, because the tissue gets better with age. Its notebooks name the behaviour, never the cause. It apologises |
| The Haven | the Quarry lot (or Josef's lot), the big one, the young by name, the stay-at-homes, the one on his own. Chalk on the signposts: *NOT AFTER 7* | The slow ones stayed home; the quick ones went with whoever called. It uses a leader's name when someone knew him, and reads the young's names at supper when they're brought home |
| Bastion | Contact Papa-two on the contact board, the heavy, new contacts, statics, a single | Contacts, counted and culled near its posts, and what it pushes away is "not our sector". "No reply" |
| The Red Hand | a crew with a nickname (the Postmen, the Choir, the Farmhands, the Commuters), the boss, new hires, the furniture, a boss who lost | Every crew's got a boss. The boss is whoever's got something on the rest. Same as them. It charges a night rate when they're out |
| The Long Table | the neighbours | *"The neighbours came past on Tuesday. We stayed in."* |

**Hard edges, always said sideways:** the Haven walks people to the edge of town at dusk, and a pack
has learned the hour; Bastion's culls push the packs into Haven country; a Red Hand crew keeps one pack
fat between its camp and Bastion, and nobody says what with; Halcyon asks people not to kill its tagged
leaders, and says sorry afterwards; the tracks round the Long Table's farm come up to the old wall and
turn back, and nobody says why; and your dead run with the pack that took them.

## Who's in charge

Every place a faction holds has someone in charge, with a name, a past and opinions:
a council head in each Haven safehouse, a commander at each Bastion post, a crew boss
for each Red Hand crew, a lead researcher at a Halcyon field station, and above them one
stronghold leader per faction (the chair of the Haven council, Bastion's commanding
officer, the boss of the Red Hand, Halcyon's research director).

Nobody outside gives these people orders any more, so they don't all follow the ones
their own side gives them either:

- **Loyal** leaders hold the stronghold's line.
- **Independent** ones run their place their own way: they bend orders to keep their
  people fed, and they remember who helped *them*.
- **Rogue** ones work around or against their stronghold: side deals with rivals,
  skimming, jobs nobody is supposed to know about.

No faction ever splits. The stronghold leaders are always loyal to their own cause, and
they never fall. Outpost and safehouse leaders are ordinary people: they get bitten, they
die in raids, and someone new takes the chair. How this works in play is in
[REPUTATION.md](REPUTATION.md), "Faction leaders".

## The Perimeter

The edge of the map is the quarantine line. **The cordon is never seen**, only felt:
a drone that drops down over you, a loudspeaker telling you to turn back, a warning shot,
then a sniper you never see. Where the main roads leave the Zone, they end in concrete
blocks and a closed barrier with guards behind it. Nobody passes, in either direction,
ever. There are no papers, bribes or deals.

There's no escape. Your character's story ends inside the Zone. Nothing crosses the line, and
nothing comes over it, but the line itself has its own small stories, rare and dangerous: a gap
in the drones' rotation that the Red Hand uses to reach the cache it hides in the kill zone, a
Haven runner's medicine dropped at the waterline when a drone found her, a body at the line
with papers on it. How the line works is in [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md), "The Perimeter"; the three
missions are in [MISSIONS.md](MISSIONS.md), "Perimeter missions". Once the cure is out the line
stands down ([Endings](#endings)).

## The outside world: silent, watching

The outside doesn't act. It doesn't send aid, doesn't bomb, doesn't negotiate.
It guards the line, and that's all. Every threat in the Zone comes from inside it.

The only sign of the outside is the **government broadcast**, looping on every
frequency. Until the cure it never changes its message, but its tone shifts with the state of
the Zone. It's flavour only, with no mechanics behind it.

What it says at each stage, and how it's written, is in [WRITING.md](WRITING.md#the-government-broadcast).

## Endings

A season is won one way: **the cure is finished** and the final stand at the lab is held.
The quarantine is lifted. The line stands down at once: the drones go home, nobody at the edge
fires again, and the loudspeakers read a new order about processing at the road checkpoints.
The barriers stay closed until the season ends, and the gates open with it: whoever walks up to
the line is told to turn around by a voice they never see, and walks back. Until then (the
epilogue) the research is over: the lab takes no more samples and nobody pays for them.

At 90% the lab's batch is missing one thing, and every faction thinks it knows what: the woman who
didn't turn, the captain's tubes, the cook's still, the lower store, or the eight who walked out.
Players race to finish it and fight for the lab's **clean room** during the stand. **The cure goes to
whoever holds the clean room when the stand is won**, and their people decide how it reaches people
([FINALE.md](FINALE.md)). When nobody holds the room, the lab hands the cure to the faction the server
gave the most Fame this season. The lab itself never falls, and neither does any stronghold:


| Cure goes to | Aftermath | Next season starts with |
|---|---|---|
| The Haven | The cure spreads person to person. Towns rebuild. | More safe towns, one more Haven safehouse |
| Bastion | Martial law. The cure only reaches those who comply. | Stronger checkpoints, a curfew, more military kit |
| The Red Hand | The cure is sold to the highest bidder. | More roadblocks, the black market open to anyone they know |
| Halcyon | The cure is published openly. | Lower infection everywhere, more specials (they adapt) |
| Nobody (the Independent route) | The cure goes to everyone, or to one side with a condition, or is sold, or kept back. | By its terms: half the Haven's and half Halcyon's, one side's at half, the black market, or nothing |

How the winner hands it out depends on the terms its people chose (the Haven's houses first or the
bite book, Bastion's roster or the column, the Red Hand's price or "pay when you can", Halcyon's
authors first or the method published). A route that finished but lost the room, or never finished,
says so in its own words, and its people keep what they were paid.

Each is a small, capped nudge; the numbers, the rule sets an admin can add (Winter, Fog,
Military Collapse, No Vehicles) and the wipe are in [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md#seasons). By default
only the cure ends a season. Only if an admin picks a timed wipe can a season end without one,
after a set number of weeks: then nobody won, and the next season starts plain.

Urgency comes from inside: the outbreak keeps slowly getting worse (stages, spread,
adaptation) until the cure is done. Even a win doesn't mean leaving: the next season is a
new outbreak, in a new Zone.
