# Setting: The Zone

The story, the people and the voice of ReforgerZ. How the systems work is in
[DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md); what each faction gives and takes is in [REPUTATION.md](REPUTATION.md).

## The premise

Eleven days ago something got out. Within forty-eight hours the government
declared the region a **Quarantine Zone** and sealed it off: roadblocks
on every road out, drones over the line, patrol boats on the water, snipers
nobody ever sees. No one gets in. **No one gets out.**

The official broadcast still loops on every frequency:

> *"This area is under federal quarantine. Remain indoors. Do not approach the
> perimeter. Aid is on its way."*

Aid is not on its way. Nothing comes in, and nothing ever will. You're a civilian who was
here when the gates closed. Everyone in the Zone is stuck in it together: the soldiers who
were left inside, the ordinary people who happened to be here, the criminals who were glad
of the chaos, and the scientists who might be the only way it ends.

**The only way the quarantine ends is a cure.**

## Every system has a reason

| System | In the Zone |
|---|---|
| Map edge | **The Perimeter.** An unseen cordon: drones, loudspeakers and snipers you never see. Get close and you're warned. Keep going and you're shot. |
| Task Force Bastion | Soldiers who were inside when the order came. Command left them here to "hold the line", and now they're as trapped as you. |
| The Haven | Ordinary people stuck in the Zone who decided to help each other, because nobody outside will. |
| The Red Hand | People who figured out that inside the Zone there are no laws, only whoever has the guns. |
| Halcyon | A research team that was studying the outbreak site before the lockdown. Only they can prove the Zone is safe, so the world outside will lift the quarantine. |
| Outbreak stages | How long the Zone has been sealed. |
| Last Helicopter | Bastion's one working helicopter, shuttling between its own bases *inside* the Zone. Nobody outside resupplies anyone, Bastion included. |
| Loot | Nothing comes in from outside. Everything you have, you scavenged, earned or took. |
| Supply trucks | Everything that moves inside the Zone moves by road. A lost truck means empty shelves. |
| The cure | Not just a cure: it's **proof**. Deliver it and the quarantine is lifted. |

## The factions, as people

### The Haven

The Haven are the people who happened to be here: a bus driver whose route ended at a
roadblock, a coach of tourists, a teacher with the eleven kids from her class trip, a
night-shift nurse, farmers, retirees. Nobody planned a faction.

It started with strangers. On Day 2 someone forced the doors of a school gym and let
people sleep there. On Day 4 a priest opened his church. Word went round by radio and
by notes on doors, and more places opened. The Haven is **a network, not an army**: a
handful of safehouses that talk to each other. The rule for getting in is simple: *you get
in if you're willing to help.*

**How a safehouse runs.** A council runs each one: whoever stepped up, usually the person
who opened the door. Every house keeps the same rules:

- You can carry and draw your weapon. **You don't fire it** near the house.
- **Everyone works**: watch, water, cooking, burying.
- **Declare your bite.** Say it out loud, and the house will help if it can.

**The Haven remembers its own.** When someone who stood up for the Haven dies, every house
lights a candle in the window and the council reads the name out at supper. Whoever turns up
later with the same look about them is fed, and seen by the medic, for nothing. Someone the
Haven has come to trust is given an amber ribbon, to wear or not as they like
([REPUTATION.md](REPUTATION.md), "Reputation clues").

**The hard edges.** The Haven is kind, and it is also frightened and hungry. It has a
little dark side that nobody talks about:

| What they don't say out loud | What it means | Where it shows in play |
|---|---|---|
| **The List** | Every house keeps a list of names. Some are quietly crossed out, and nobody says why. | Rumours and notes; The List contract (why one name was crossed out, and who gets told) |
| **"Walking someone out"** | What happens to someone who declared a bite. It differs by house: in one they sit with you until the end; in another someone walks you to the edge of town at dusk and comes back alone. | The Walk Him Out contract |
| **"Mouths"** | When food runs low, people who can't work become "mouths", and mouths get smaller portions. | Half portions in the news; the Quiet Congregation arc |
| **Exile** | Break the rules badly enough and you're put out at night, without your weapon. | Exile when the Haven hunts you |
| **Quiet arrangements** | Some houses pay the Red Hand to be left alone. Sometimes the price is a stranger: someone the house turned out, and where to find them. | A Quiet Arrangement; a hungry house selling an exile's location |
| **"Borrowing"** | Taking from houses that weren't empty. Taking from a better-fed safehouse. Always in quotes. | Haven gossip in the news |

**How they see the others:**

- **Bastion** is useful and frightening. They have medicine and guns, and they have orders.
- **The Red Hand** were ordinary people once. Some of them used to come to the same church.
- **Halcyon** calls the infected "samples". The Haven calls them by name when it can.

**Voice:** warm, worried, practical, sometimes darkly funny. *"Bring water if you can. Bring
yourself either way."*

### Task Force Bastion

Soldiers who were inside the line when the order came: a battalion on exercise in the hills,
the first cordon detail sent in to "contain an incident", reservists, a few men on leave who
reported for duty anyway. Command told them to hold the line and stopped answering. They still
run checkpoints, still keep a duty roster, still call Command on the radio every night. Some
still follow orders. Some are starting to wonder whose side they're on.

It started with orders that made sense. On Day 1 the cordon detail put roadblocks round an
"incident" in the hills and was told it was a two-week posting. At six on the morning of Day 2
the order came to walk civilians to the road checkpoints at the edge, to be processed out.
Lieutenant Hana Sedlak took a bus, forty cars and a line of people on foot to the checkpoint
on the north road. At two in the afternoon the barrier was still down. A loudspeaker told them
to turn back. The soldiers behind the concrete wore the same badge she did. When she walked
up to talk to them, a warning shot went through the bus's mirror. She turned the column round.
The bus driver's route ended there, and he went looking for somewhere to put his passengers.

That evening the last clear message came over the net: *"Hold the line. Await relief."*
On Day 3 a longer signal came through the relay, too broken for voice, and a runner set off
with the decoded text. He never arrived. That night the senior officer inside, a major, gave
the units a name on the radio, Task Force Bastion, so that Command would have something to
call them. Command never used it. The same night a pilot flew for the line with the wounded
aboard. The line shot him down short of it. Nobody has flown toward the edge since, and of
the helicopters Bastion came in with, only one still flies.

**How a post runs.** A commander runs each post: an officer if there's one left, a sergeant
if not. Every post keeps the same routine:

- **The roster.** Every name, every duty, in grease pencil on a board. The shift changes at
  05:30 and 17:30, and the night watch starts at 20:30.
- **The nightly call.** After the night watch is set, the radio operator reads the numbers to
  Command: effective strength, rounds, fuel, civilians sheltered. Then they wait. Then they
  write *"No reply"* in the log, and the sergeant chalks up one more day.
- **Inside the wire,** weapons are slung and nobody fires. Outside it, civilians are searched
  at the checkpoints and there's a curfew at dusk.
- **Everything is signed for.** Ammunition, rations, fuel, people.

On the secure net, the stronghold answers to "Command" now. Someone had to.

**Bastion counts its dead.** They're buried outside the post with no salute, because they're
saving ammunition. One tag stays with the body. The other goes on a nail behind the radio, and
that night the operator reads the new names to Command along with the numbers. Promotions
come off the dead too: the new corporal's stripes were cut from a dead man's sleeve, and
nobody thinks less of them for it. A civilian who has earned Bastion's trust is waved
through at the checkpoints, and a few are trusted to throw the smoke the guns answer to
([REPUTATION.md](REPUTATION.md), "Task Force Bastion").

**The hard edges.** Bastion is disciplined, and the discipline is all that holds it together.
Underneath, it is doing things nobody would put in a report:

| What they don't say out loud | What it means | Where it shows in play |
|---|---|---|
| **"No reply"** | Every night the log says the same thing. Some operators swear they've heard something on the net. Nobody writes that down. | The nightly call on Bastion's open channel; the rumour that Bastion has a radio to the outside; No Reply, where something answers on the Command net and you decide what the log says (it is never Command, and who it was is never said); the Await Relief questline, where the broken burst after the call turns out to be Bastion's own Day 3 signal, still looping off a relay nobody acknowledged ([QUESTLINES.md](QUESTLINES.md#b1-await-relief)) |
| **"Expended"** | Nobody knows what the last orders said. Some posts think "relief is coming". Others have heard a word they don't repeat. Any copy that reaches the stronghold goes into the stove. | The Real Orders; the Day 3 runner's page in Await Relief |
| **"Struck off"** | A deserter's name is struck off the roster and written up as killed in action, so the numbers add up when relief comes. Then someone is sent to find him. | Hunt the Deserter; Desertion Route; Struck Off, where a man on the roster as killed in action turns up alive (the Deserter wanderer knows where he sleeps); the rumour that Bastion shoots its own deserters |
| **The bunker** | Stores that Bastion never told anyone about, not even its own posts. | The Deserter's Map arc |
| **"Contribution"** | Food taken from civilians "for their protection". It's never called a tax, and on a bad week it's never written down. | Requisition; Off-Book Requisition |
| **"Fell off the inventory"** | Rifles that went to the Red Hand for fuel and fresh food. Ledgers where rations "spoil" and diesel "evaporates". | Rifles for Sale; Burn the Ledger |
| **Containment** | Bastion does to a town what was done to Bastion: barriers across the roads, nobody in or out. Or the guns, after five minutes' warning, whoever is still inside. | Quarantine Line; Bastion Shelling; the Fire Mission perk |

**How they see the others:**

- **The Haven** are the people Bastion walked to the line and back on Day 2. Every post feeds
  them a little and resents it a little.
- **The Red Hand** are looters with a logo. Bastion keeps a few of them in a stockade, and the rest
  keep trying to get them out. They laugh at Bastion on the open channel, and Bastion keeps a list.
- **Halcyon** has the lab Command wanted secured. Bastion thinks Halcyon knew what was coming
  before anyone, and would like the cure somewhere it can count it.

**Voice:** clipped, procedural, tired. *"Command, Bastion. Effective strength thirty-eight.
Civilians sheltered, two hundred and eleven. Requesting relief. Over."* A long hiss. *"Bastion out."*

### The Red Hand

People who stopped pretending. Some were criminals before; most were mechanics, drivers and
bored young men who noticed that nobody was coming. They take tolls on the roads, run a
black market and sell protection. They don't care who you were. They care who you've crossed.

It started with a tank of diesel. On Day 1 the guards at the prison went home to their
families, and by morning the cells were open. On Day 2 the cars that had been turned back from
the line queued at every petrol station, and the pumps ran dry. Rudi Kasper drove the tow
truck for a garage at a crossroads, and he had the key to the tank under the forecourt. He sold
diesel for money, until money stopped meaning anything. Then he sold it for tins.

On Day 4 the Janda brothers parked an articulated lorry across the crossroads. Rudi put his hand
in the red paint they used for the forecourt lines and slapped it on the trailer. *"Red hand.
Like at the lights. Means stop."* The first toll was one tin a car. On Day 5 a Bastion truck
came to clear the road. The driver looked at the lorry, at the lads on top of it and at his
fuel gauge, and paid in diesel. It's the Red Hand's favourite story. Bastion says it never
happened.

The convicts drifted in that week, and then the rest. Other crossroads got lorries and painted
hands. Rudi was bitten on Day 8. He sat on the lorry with a bottle and asked them to do it
before dark, and they did. The crews argued for two days about who was boss. The boss is the
one who won.

**How it runs.** A crew holds a camp and a stretch of road, and a crew boss runs it. Every
crew pays **the boss** a cut, and the boss doesn't get a say in anything else. The boss runs
**the market** at the stronghold: stolen kit, a fence who buys watches and jewellery by the
handful, and the cut dose. On the roads there's **the toll**: tins, fuel or manners, mostly
tins. Everything owed is in **the book**, and the book has to balance.

The code is short, and everyone knows it:

- **Pay your cut.** Crews that skim get found out, eventually.
- **A debt is a debt.** It stays in the book until it's paid, by someone.
- **Guns stay quiet in camp.** You want to shoot someone, do it outside.
- **Nobody rats.** Not to Bastion, not to anyone.
- **They don't care who you were.** They care who you've crossed, and they remember.

**The Red Hand sends its own off loud.** Horns, music, shots in the air, whatever's left in
the bottle. More than once a horde has come to see what the fuss was. The dead man's boots go
to whoever carried him home. What he owed dies with him; what he was owed, the crew collects
in his name. Status is a **name**: you don't pick your own, the crew gives you one, usually
after something stupid. New faces paint a red hand on, and the ones the crews trust get the
red armband ([REPUTATION.md](REPUTATION.md), "Reputation clues").

**The hard edges.** The Red Hand jokes about everything. That's how it avoids saying anything:

| What they don't say out loud | What it means | Where it shows in play |
|---|---|---|
| **The cut dose** | Antidote stretched with whatever was in the bag. They know it works half the time. They sell it to outsiders, never to the crew. | The cut dose on the black market |
| **"The well story"** | What happens to a place that doesn't pay is never said. Somebody tells the story about the well instead, and lets you finish it yourself. | Protection Racket; the poisoned-well rumours, Fever Town's among them |
| **Strays** | Crews keep track of the people the Haven turned out, and where they sleep. A hungry safehouse will sometimes sell one. | A hungry house selling an exile's location; A Quiet Arrangement |
| **Learning the drones** | The gap in the drones' rotation was found by sending people toward the line and timing what happened. Most of the ones they sent were in the book. | The Blind Spot (the gap); Learning the Drones (how they found it) |
| **The pit** | A ring of infected and bets on who stays standing. When nobody volunteers, someone in the book goes in. | Fight Pit and House Rules (the pit); The Pit's Debtor |
| **"Cargo"** | Trucks that carry people, not tins. Where to, nobody says. | Cargo (the Haven follows a truck to a shed); rumours |
| **Crews eat crews** | Out loud there's one Red Hand. Quietly, crews sell each other's camps to Bastion, and the boss purges any crew that talks about a new boss. | Sell Out a Rival Crew; Coup Talk |

**How they see the others:**

- **The Haven:** lovely people, lots of tins, terrible locks. Half the crews have a mother or a
  kid sister in a safehouse, and some crew bosses quietly send medicine.
- **Bastion** is still saluting a radio that stopped talking. Bastion paid the first toll.
- **Halcyon** pays in hope; the Red Hand pays in cash. The lab's doses sell better with the
  lab's stickers still on.

**Voice:** mocking and casual. They laugh at Bastion on the open channel. *"Red hand means stop.
Stop means pay. Pay means we're friends. See? Easy."*

### Halcyon

A research team that was at the outbreak site before the lockdown. They run the only lab.
They pay for samples, they need guards, and they know more about how this started than they
say. A cure is the only thing that opens the gates, for them and for everyone.

It started before Day 1, and that's the problem. Halcyon is the field team of a research
institute, and it had been working for months at a closed site in the hills. What the site
was for is in their reports. Nobody else has read the reports. Three weeks before the gates
closed, a man from the site was brought into a clinic with a fever nobody could name. Someone
wrote *"notify the Institute"* in the margin and underlined it twice.

On Day 1, whatever got out, got out. The team loaded two vans with the freezers, the
centrifuge and every notebook they could find, and drove down into town. On Day 2 the
Institute's last call reached them: nobody leaves until the Zone is proven clean, and proof
opens the gates. Then the line went dead. On Day 3 the team lead, Dr Greta Wolff, went back up
to the site for the rest of the samples. Her driver came back alone and wouldn't say what he'd
seen. On Day 4 a Bastion platoon arrived with Command's order, two days old, to secure the
lab. The researchers wouldn't open the doors, and the soldiers wouldn't leave, and they stood
there all night. In the morning
the platoon went home, except for two men who stayed and became Halcyon's first guards. On Day 6
ten bitten people from the Haven volunteered for the first trial dose. Eight walked out. The
number has been eight in ten ever since, which the lab says is a coincidence.

**How it runs.** The research director runs the lab, and the lab never stops:

- **The lab** works in shifts, because somebody always has to watch the incubators. At night
  the generator goes to the freezers and the lights go dim. The overnight numbers are read out
  at the change of shift. Nobody comments on them.
- **The field stations.** A house in a quiet village with sensors, a cold box and a radio, run
  by a lead researcher who is often younger than their job. Couriers walk samples between them
  and the lab in steel cases.
- **The trial dose.** Anyone bitten may volunteer, once, for nothing. Eight in ten walk out,
  and Halcyon writes down what happens to the other two
  ([DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md#treatments)).
- **The notebooks.** Everyone keeps one, numbered. Every reading, every sample, every
  volunteer goes in it. When a researcher dies, the notebook belongs to the lab.
- **The numbers go out on the open channel,** with the samples wanted, in case somebody
  outside is writing them down.

**Halcyon keeps its dead in the record.** When someone dies, the director speaks for two
minutes and goes back to work. Their notebook is read into the record, page by page, and their
name goes on the list of authors for the paper that will open the gates: researchers, couriers,
guards. Volunteers who didn't walk out are on it too, by number. The Haven wants them named, and now and
then somebody carries a name back up the hill.
Status is whose initials go on a result, and who is let into the clean room. An outsider the lab
trusts gets antidotes, a medic, and in the end a pass to the lab itself
([REPUTATION.md](REPUTATION.md), "Halcyon").

**The hard edges.** Halcyon is careful, polite and very tired. It has decided some things
matter more than people, and it apologises for that, quietly:

| What they don't say out loud | What it means | Where it shows in play |
|---|---|---|
| **The three weeks** | The first case was three weeks before the gates closed, and the Institute was told. Nobody at the lab says what anyone did with those three weeks. | Patient Zero (the clinic records); The Three Weeks (the closed site's gate log and the Institute's reply: "carry on, say nothing"); the rumour that Halcyon made this |
| **"The other two"** | The two in ten who don't walk out of the trial dose leave with half the time they came in with, and a page in someone's notebook. Someone sits with them with a clipboard instead of a hand. | The trial dose; Serum Trial; Unsanctioned Trial; The Author List (one of them named, from a Haven safehouse) |
| **"Samples first, people second"** | When a van can't take both, the cold boxes go. It's an order, and they say sorry for it on the secure channel. | Secure channels |
| **"At the moment of turning"** | The protocol wants tissue taken as a person turns, so someone has to be there, waiting, with the kit. | Patients Not Samples |
| **Live ones** | Specials caught alive, for the lab, and sometimes for a field lead's own notebook. People say Halcyon pays the Red Hand for live ones. | Live Capture; Private Collection; rumours |
| **Pages in the stove** | A dead researcher's notebook belongs to the lab. The pages about the director don't always get there. | The Notebook |
| **Lazarus** | A locked terminal from a team that went into the field and didn't come back. Nobody at the lab will say what the project was. | The Last Experiment |

**How they see the others:**

- **The Haven** keeps people alive long enough to matter. Its medics have seen more bites than
  anyone at the lab, and the lab sometimes asks them what it's getting wrong.
- **Bastion** would like the cure in a locked crate. The director would trade what the lab
  knows for Bastion's protection, and some field leads would rather give it away.
- **The Red Hand** buys samples, and every one it buys is research the lab loses. It also sells
  the lab the things the director won't ask for.

**Voice:** precise, a bit cold, sometimes guilty. *"Eight in ten. We write down the other two.
Somebody has to, and they should be counted."*

## The Long Table

*Planned, not built.* How the house works in the world is in [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md#the-long-table-planned);
how players deal with it is in [REPUTATION.md](REPUTATION.md#the-long-table-planned); its two
questlines are in [QUESTLINES.md](QUESTLINES.md#the-long-table).

Not a faction. A family of four to seven people in one farmhouse, in one of the empty places
nobody goes. They're kind to strangers. There's always an extra place set at supper. And they
eat people.

It started with the Haven's mouths. On Day 3 Ada Moravec opened her farmhouse past the last
village to anyone the other houses couldn't feed. She had two grown sons, a daughter-in-law and a
kitchen table long enough for twenty. The council sent her its mouths: the old, the sick, a man in
a wheelchair, two men who couldn't stop coughing, and a boy of ten whose mother never made it to
the gym. On Day 7 the Haven truck to the farm didn't come. On Day 8 the council's radio said there
would be nothing for the farm that week: houses with workers came first. On Day 9 old Mr Halas
died in the night. In the morning there was soup. Ada said grace and read his name out, the way
every Haven house reads its dead at supper. Nobody asked. The ones who would have asked weren't
strong enough to.

On Day 10 one of the coughing men turned out to have a bite he hadn't declared, and by the next
night Ada's daughter-in-law was sick. Her husband walked her out at dusk and came back alone. The
family has asked every guest about bites ever since. By Day 12 the farm had stopped answering the
radio. The council crossed the whole house off the List and said a prayer for it, and everyone
agreed the infected must have got there first.

The mouths are gone now. The family is still there, and the boy.

**How the house runs.** Ada runs the kitchen, and the kitchen is the house:

- **There's always a place set.** An extra bowl at the long table, for whoever comes.
- **Everyone is welcome.** Her elder son, the host, meets travellers on the road with a flask of
  something hot. He knows the roads, the trucks and who's short, and everything he says is true.
- **Declare your bite.** They ask every guest, kindly, the way a Haven house does. A bitten guest
  is fed, blessed and sent on their way.
- **Grace.** Before supper Ada reads a name.
- **Nothing is wasted.** That's the whole of the reason, and they say it often.

They tell themselves they only took the ones who were going anyway. That was true for a week.

**The hard edges.** The house is homely, and that's the horror of it:

| What they don't say out loud | What it means | Where it shows in play (planned) |
|---|---|---|
| **Grace** | At a Haven house, the name read at supper is the dead. At Ada's, it's the meal | The host's invitation; A Place at the Table |
| **"Nothing is wasted"** | It started with the dead. Then the dying. Then the ones nobody would come looking for | Wanderers who never arrive; rumours |
| **"Declare your bite"** | Asked kindly, like a Haven house. A bitten guest is safe: they learned on Day 10 what the bitten do to a family | A bitten player is never set on; Halcyon's interest |
| **The guestbook** | Every guest signs. Some names have a date and a small cross beside them, the way the List does | Proof in The House That Went Quiet |
| **The boots** | A row of boots by the door, in too many sizes | A tell at the house |
| **"Pork"** | What the jars are, when they're sold. The one Red Hand crew that buys them doesn't ask | Trade; Cargo |
| **Strays** | Some of the people the Haven turned out, that the Red Hand kept track of, were sold on here | Cargo; The Price of the Door |
| **The boy** | He isn't theirs. He came with the mouths. He eats what he's given and doesn't talk about it | The end of the house; the Kid wanderer |

**How the others see them** (once they know):

- **The Haven:** horror, and shame. It was one of their houses, and they sent it their mouths and
  then sent it nothing. The Haven calls it "the house that went quiet" and never says more. Its
  instinct is to bring them home, and walk them out.
- **Bastion:** a line in a report: an infected cluster past the last village, cleared. It doesn't
  fit anywhere on the board, so the commanding officer would rather burn it than count it. A
  post or two has bought venison from a man with a flask.
- **The Red Hand:** the code has five rules and none of them is about this. The boss says it
  doesn't need one. One crew buys the jars, calls it pork and sells the farm strays; most crews
  would hang the family off the lorry if they knew.
- **Halcyon:** interested, and ashamed of being interested. The family never eats a bitten guest,
  because of Day 10. The lab would like to know if they're right: whether eating the infected
  spreads it. It would like one of them alive to ask, and a sample to be sure.

**Voice:** homely, attentive, a little too interested in you. *"Sit, sit. You're thin. Where've you
come from, and does anyone know you're out this way?"* Then grace: *"For what we are about to
receive. For Tomas, who came from Levie."*

**Writing them.** Never describe it. No kitchen work, no cellar contents, no word for what they
are, from them or from the narrator. The horror is in ordinary things: the extra bowl, the boots,
the names, the jars labelled by the day and not by what's in them. Other people may joke (the Red
Hand says "pork"); the writing never does.

## Who's in charge

Every place a faction holds has someone in charge, with a name, a past and opinions:
a council head in each Haven safehouse, a commander at each Bastion post, a crew boss
for each Red Hand crew, a lead researcher at a Halcyon field station, and above them one
stronghold leader per faction (the chair of the Haven council, Bastion's commanding
officer, the boss of the Red Hand, Halcyon's research director).

Nobody outside gives these people orders any more, so they don't all follow the ones
their own side gives them either:

- **Loyal** leaders hold the stronghold's line.
- **Independent** ones run their place their own way: they bend orders to keep their
  people fed, and they remember who helped *them*.
- **Rogue** ones work around or against their stronghold: side deals with rivals,
  skimming, jobs nobody is supposed to know about.

No faction ever splits. The stronghold leaders are always loyal to their own cause, and
they never fall. Outpost and safehouse leaders are ordinary people: they get bitten, they
die in raids, and someone new takes the chair. How this works in play is in
[REPUTATION.md](REPUTATION.md), "Faction leaders".

## The Perimeter

The edge of the map is the quarantine line. **The cordon is never seen**, only felt:
a drone that drops down over you, a loudspeaker telling you to turn back, a warning shot,
then a sniper you never see. Where the main roads leave the Zone, they end in concrete
blocks and a closed barrier with guards behind it. Nobody passes, in either direction,
ever. There are no papers, bribes or deals.

There's no escape. Your character's story ends inside the Zone. Nothing crosses the line, and
nothing comes over it, but the line itself has its own small stories, rare and dangerous: a gap
in the drones' rotation that the Red Hand uses to reach the cache it hides in the kill zone, a
Haven runner's medicine dropped at the waterline when a drone found her, a body at the line
with papers on it. How the line works is in [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md), "The Perimeter"; the three
missions are in [MISSIONS.md](MISSIONS.md), "Perimeter missions".

## The outside world: silent, watching

The outside doesn't act. It doesn't send aid, doesn't bomb, doesn't negotiate.
It guards the line, and that's all. Every threat in the Zone comes from inside it.

The only sign of the outside is the **government broadcast**, looping on every
frequency. It never changes its message, but its tone shifts with the state of
the Zone. It's flavour only, with no mechanics behind it:

| Zone state | Broadcast tone |
|---|---|
| Early, cure progressing | *"Remain indoors. Aid is on its way."* |
| Infection spreading, cure stalled | *"Residents are reminded that the perimeter is for your protection."* |
| Overrun, little progress | *"The perimeter will be maintained."* |
| Long stall | *"The perimeter will be maintained indefinitely."* |
| Cure breakthroughs | *"Reports of research inside the zone are being evaluated."* |

## Endings

A season is won one way: **the cure is finished** and the final stand at the lab is held.
The quarantine is lifted and the gates open. The cure goes to the faction the server gave
the most Fame this season, and that faction decides how it reaches people:

| Cure goes to | Aftermath | Next season starts with |
|---|---|---|
| The Haven | The cure spreads person to person. Towns rebuild. | More safe towns, one more Haven safehouse |
| Bastion | Martial law. The cure only reaches those who comply. | Stronger checkpoints, a curfew, more military kit |
| The Red Hand | The cure is sold to the highest bidder. | More roadblocks, the black market open to anyone they know |
| Halcyon | The cure is published openly. | Lower infection everywhere, more specials (they adapt) |

Each is a small, capped nudge; the numbers, the rule sets an admin can add (Winter, Fog,
Military Collapse, No Vehicles) and the wipe are in [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md#seasons). A season can
also end without a cure when the server wipes after a set number of weeks: then nobody won, and
the next season starts plain.

Urgency comes from inside: the outbreak keeps slowly getting worse (stages, spread,
adaptation) until the cure is done. Even a win doesn't mean leaving: the next season is a
new outbreak, in a new Zone.

## Tone

- **Bleak but human.** The world is cruel. People are what make it worth surviving.
- **No heroes from outside.** No one is coming. The broadcasts are lies or useless.
- **Ordinary people.** You're a nurse, a mechanic, a kid out of work. Not a commando.
- **Small stories.** A note on a fridge, a radio message cut off, a body near the line.
- **Nobody is a villain.** Every faction does something ugly for a reason you can understand.

## Writing guide

- **Broadcasts** are short, official and slightly wrong:
  *"Day 14. Residents are reminded that the perimeter is for your protection."*
- **Notes** are personal and specific: names, places in the town they're found in, and mistakes.
- **Faction voices:**
  - **Bastion:** clipped, procedural, tired.
  - **Haven:** warm, worried, practical, sometimes darkly funny.
  - **Red Hand:** mocking and casual.
  - **Halcyon:** precise, a bit cold, sometimes guilty.
  - **The Long Table** (planned): homely, attentive, a little too interested in you.
- **Open channels** on the radio are people, not institutions. The Haven's is a tired woman:
  *"If you can hear this, Morton is open. Bring water if you can. Bring yourself either way."*
  Bastion's net still calls Command, which never answers. The Red Hand uses the open channel
  to laugh at Bastion: *"Still waiting for relief? We'll keep the kettle on."*
- **Faction chatter** on the open channels is shift talk with something real in it: a truck
  leaving in twenty minutes, a toll until dusk, the night watch at 20:30. People say more on the
  radio than they should.
- **Rumours** are gossip, often wrong and worst about rivals: *"I heard Bastion got thrown out
  of Levie. Good riddance."* Never mark which ones are true.
- **Things found in drawers and on bodies** (ID cards, letters, receipts, photos) are one person's
  life in a sentence or two: a name, the town they're found in, what they bought or meant to say.
  *"A till receipt from Morton, Day 2: tinned peaches, batteries (all of them), bleach. Underneath,
  in pencil: 'enough for a week?'"* Some point somewhere: a key with a tag, a grid on the back.
- **Secure channels** are what a faction says when it thinks nobody's listening: blunter than its
  open channel, with numbers in it. *"All stations: Morton rotates the watch at 20:30. Two on the
  gate. Ten minutes where nobody's looking."*
- **Headlines** about the factions are one plain sentence with one small detail: *"Bastion buried
  Corporal Weber outside Morton. No salute: they're saving ammunition."*
- **Haven gossip** is about food, never blame: *"The safehouse in Levie has gone to half portions.
  The children eat first."* When a hungry house takes from a fed one, it's *"borrowed"*, in quotes.
  The Haven's dark side is always said sideways, never announced.
- **Players on the radio station** pick a preset, and it reads like a person: their name, one real
  place, one request. *"This is Mara. There's a horde moving around Morton. Stay clear of it."*
- **Leaders** speak in their faction's voice, coloured by their traits: a Greedy one talks
  about pay first, a Paranoid one asks who saw you come in, a Merciful one tells you to sit
  down and eat. One short line before a brief and one after, never a speech.
