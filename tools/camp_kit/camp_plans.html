Camp piece plans

Top-down plans for the 54 camp-life pieces, richer and less regular: props turned at odd angles, stacked, leaned and left where people put them. Each plan is to scale on a 1 m grid; the arrow marks the piece's front. Orange dots are where a person stands or sits, with the way they face. Hover a shape for its prop.

Drawn from tools/camp_kit/pieces.py, the file the prefabs are built from (build.py), so the plans and the game match. Decals (the red hand, the bear, the plague doctor) are still planned, so their boards are plain for now.

How each faction builds

Holdings sit in real towns, so a faction moves into the building that suits what it does and fortifies around it. Camp life picks that building in each town by kind and size (the school, town hall or police station for Bastion, the garage or petrol station for the Red Hand, the church or a big house for the Haven, the lab or a house for Halcyon) and lays its pieces out in the yard, along the walls and at the road entries (docs/DESIGN.md, Camp life). These plans are schematic, and show more than is built: the rooms inside, the roof lookouts, the graves, the stockade, the dogs and the searchlight are not (the doc lists what is simplified).

Task Force Bastion

Soldiers who were told to hold the line and stayed. They don't build a camp, they occupy: the most solid public building in a town on the roads, with the roads in closed by sandbags and wire. Everything is signed for, and the radio room never stops calling Command.

Stronghold: the school on the main road

People
Roster of about 40 (30 soldiers, 10 civilians attached). In play: 8 to 10 guards, the major, the operator, the cook and the sentry.
Where
The largest stone building in a bigger town on a crossroads or near an airfield: a school, a town hall or a police station. Bastion needs walls, a roof for a lookout, and a road for the truck.
For
Command for every post: the roster, the radio, the stores everything is signed out of, the motor pool for the one truck route, the stockade.
Defence
Sandbag nests with wire on every road into the town (a gate on the main road, the others closed), a lookout on the school roof, the flagless pole in the yard, a security-fence gate at the school entrance, curfew at dusk and a searchlight sweep at night.
  • HQ and radio room. The school office. The roster board, the map, the operator on the set calling "No reply" every night, the nails behind the radio with the dog tags of the dead.
  • Barracks. The gym and two classrooms: cots in rows, kit at the foot of each, a duty rota on the door.
  • Stores. A locked classroom: ammunition, rations and diesel, every crate chalked with a count. The quartermaster's window.
  • Motor pool. The yard behind the school: the truck, jerrycans under a camo net, a workbench.
  • Mess. The canteen: the dusk meal at 18:30, the cook, tables pushed together.
  • Aid post. The nurse's room: a medic, a stretcher, the bitten soldier's chair.
  • Stockade. The bike shed fenced off with wire: whoever Bastion is holding.
  • Graves. Outside the wall, a row of crosses with one tag each, no salute.

Outpost: the farm at the junction

People
A section of 8 to 12 ("eleven men at a post past the hills"). In play: 4 to 6 guards, the sergeant, the sentry.
Where
A farmhouse or a petrol station where two roads meet, between the stronghold and a town Bastion watches.
For
Hold the road, search civilians, send the stronghold "rations, one day" and wait for the truck.
Defence
One nest on each road, a barrier on the main one, sandbags round the farmhouse door, one man on the hay loft with binoculars.
  • Farmhouse. Bunks upstairs, the radio and the roster chalked on a kitchen door.
  • Barn. Stores and the truck when it comes.
  • Yard. Fire pit, table, shaving stand, the flag pole.
  • Road post. A barrier and a nest on the junction.

The Red Hand

Crews holding a stretch of road for the toll. They take what was already useful: a garage, a petrol station, a scrapyard at a crossroads. Nothing is planned; everything is dragged into place. The book is kept in the office, and nobody fires a gun in camp.

Stronghold: Rudi's garage at the crossroads

People
The boss's crew of about 25, plus whoever is trading. In play: 6 to 8 crew, the boss, the fence, the toll man, the cook.
Where
A garage or petrol station on a crossroads at the edge of a small town, with some infection nearby to keep the curious away.
For
The market, the book, and the toll on four roads. Crews come here to pay their cut.
Defence
Wrecks dragged across every road but one, the toll barrier on that one, a lookout on the garage roof, scrap walls closing the gaps, dogs, and the red hand painted where you can't miss it.
  • The office. The boss's desk and the book. Nobody goes in without being asked.
  • The market. The garage forecourt under the canopy: stalls of stolen kit, the fence buying watches and rings.
  • Chop shop. The workshop: cars on blocks, parts on the walls, the stolen ones being stripped.
  • Bunk house. The house behind the garage: mattresses, the sofa, bottles.
  • The pit. A fenced corner with the dogs: they bark before anyone gets close.
  • Trophy wall. Corrugated iron by the road hung with Bastion helmets, number plates and skulls on boards.

Outpost: a crew camp on the road

People
One crew of 4 to 8 and its boss. In play: 3 to 4 crew, the toll man.
Where
A lay-by, a roadside café or a scrapyard on a road between towns, where traffic has to slow.
For
Take the toll, sell what they take, and keep something on every other crew.
Defence
The barrier, a scrap nest with a view down the road, a tin sheet with the red hand facing traffic.
  • Café or shed. Where the crew sleeps and the boss keeps the crew's page of the book.
  • Fire barrel and sofa. The camp is around the fire.
  • Toll barrier. Logs on tyres, a chair, a barrel fire.

The Haven

Ordinary people who opened doors for each other. Their places are the ones that were open to everyone before: the church, the school gym, a big farmhouse. They barricade, they don't fortify, and almost nobody carries a gun.

Stronghold: the church and its hall

People
About 60 people, families and old people among them. In play: 3 to 4 watchers, the council head, the priest, the cook, children and occupants.
Where
A quiet village with a church, away from the main roads and from the Red Hand, low infection.
For
Shelter and food for anyone who works, the List, the radio that tells the safehouses what's out there.
Defence
Boarded ground-floor windows, the bear stencilled on the gate and the door boards, a watcher in the bell tower who rings it, fences and carts across the lanes, a few hunters with rifles.
  • The church. Where people sleep: pews pushed aside, mattresses in rows, the bell in the tower as the alarm.
  • Church hall. The kitchen and the dusk meal, long tables, the council's table at the end.
  • The List and the radio. In the vestry: the board of names, the radio set, the map with safehouses pinned.
  • Sick room. A side room where someone who has declared a bite sits with a friend.
  • Store. The crypt or a barn: tins counted on a slate, seed, water.
  • Garden and yard. Vegetable beds in the churchyard, the washing lines, a rain barrel.
  • Memorial. Candles and names on the church steps.

Safehouse: a house that kept its door open

People
8 to 15 people. In play: 2 occupants and the house's head.
Where
Any quiet house in a village nobody holds: two storeys, a garden, a door that locks.
For
A bed, a meal, a radio call to the church, and somewhere to declare a bite.
Defence
Boards, a dog, and a rule that nobody opens the door after dark.
  • House. Everyone sleeps upstairs; the kitchen table is where everything happens.
  • Garden. The bed of vegetables, the line, the barrel.
  • Door. Boards on the windows, the bear on the door board.

Halcyon

A research team running the only lab. They need power, cold and quiet. Everything is about keeping samples cold and clean: generators, freezers, a decon line, and couriers with steel cases.

Stronghold: the lab

People
About 20: the director, 8 researchers, couriers, a medic and 6 guards (two of them the soldiers who stayed on Day 5). In play: 3 to 4 guards, the director, the medic, a courier.
Where
Where the game puts the lab: an institute or clinic building, ideally on high ground outside a town.
For
The cure. The clean room, the freezers, the trial dose, the numbers read out on the open channel.
Defence
A chain-link security fence with one gate through the decon line, floodlights on the generator, guards at the gate and on the roof, warning signs and the plague-doctor stencil.
  • Clean room. The inner room. Only the trusted get in.
  • Freezers and incubators. On the generator at night; the lights dim so they don't.
  • Trial ward. Where volunteers take the dose, and where the two in ten are written down.
  • Decon line. At the only way in: the shower, the bins for clothes, the rule.
  • Notebook room. Every notebook, numbered; the dead ones' too.
  • Power. Two generators in a fenced yard, fuel barrels, cables everywhere.
  • Antenna. The mast for the open channel and the couriers.

Field station: a house in a quiet village

People
3 to 5: a lead researcher, an assistant, a courier, sometimes a guard. In play: the researcher and one other.
Where
A house at the edge of a quiet, low-infection village, near ground where the packs are being watched.
For
Sensors, a cold box, a radio; samples walked to the lab in steel cases.
Defence
Almost none: the plague-doctor board at the gate and the hope that nobody wants what's inside.
  • House. The front room is the lab bench; the notebooks are on the kitchen table.
  • Yard. The generator, the antenna, the decon shower, the cold box.

How a camp hangs together

The plan of every kit, the game data itself (pieces.py, KITS). The game lays a plan down whole: on open ground turned to face the nearest road, by a building in its yard with the plan’s front against the wall (the approach pieces go to the road entries, the door pieces to the front door, no tents), at a road post beside the road. A piece that doesn’t fit is nudged a little or left out, never moved somewhere else. The counter stands wherever the quartermaster does.
